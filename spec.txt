1. Executive Summary
tpt-teleop-marine is the hyper-optimized, zero-bloat, Rust-based middleware workspace designed to operate in the most physically hostile and communication-starved environment on Earth: the ocean.
While tpt-teleop-fleet orchestrates 2D warehouse floors with millimeter WiFi precision, and tpt-t-agri handles hectare-scale fields with RTK GPS, tpt-teleop-marine tackles full 3D navigation without GPS, acoustic communication at 100 bits per second, and pressure environments that would crush a human. It orchestrates AUVs (Autonomous Underwater Vehicles), ROVs (Remotely Operated Vehicles), ASVs (Autonomous Surface Vessels), and gliders for offshore wind inspection, subsea pipeline survey, aquaculture, defense, and scientific oceanography.
The fundamental challenges are unique: radio waves do not penetrate water, GPS does not exist below the surface, sound travels at 1500 m/s (introducing 666ms latency per kilometer), and a single seal failure means the loss of a $2 million vehicle. This spec is built around those constraints.

3. Workspace Architecture (The Crates)
The repository is structured as a Cargo Workspace. Every crate uses the simplified tpt-t-marine- prefix.
Core & 3D Navigation
tpt-t-marine-core: The central event loop, mission state machine (Dive → Transit → Survey → Surface → Recover), and lock-free message bus.
tpt-t-marine-nav: GPS-denied 3D navigation. Fuses INS (Inertial Navigation System), DVL (Doppler Velocity Log), pressure depth sensors, and acoustic positioning (USBL/LBL/SBL) via a custom zero-allocation Extended Kalman Filter.
tpt-t-marine-buoyancy: Unique to marine. Manages variable ballast systems, trim pumps, and buoyancy engines. Handles the physics of compression at depth (vehicles get heavier as neoprene compresses).
tpt-t-marine-acoustic: Acoustic modem integration. Custom ultra-low-bandwidth protocol stack for WHOI Micro-Modem, EvoLogics, and LinkQuest. Handles the brutal reality of 60% packet loss and multi-path fading.
Vehicle-Specific Execution
tpt-t-marine-auv: Autonomous underwater vehicle mission execution. Waypoint following in 3D space, adaptive sampling (deciding where to go next based on sensor data), and "sleep-wake" power management for multi-month deployments.
tpt-t-marine-rov: Tethered ROV control. 6DOF thruster coordination, manipulator arm control, and real-time video/telemetry over fiber-optic tethers.
tpt-t-marine-asv: Autonomous surface vessel control. Waypoint following, dynamic positioning (DP), and station-keeping in currents and waves.
tpt-t-marine-glider: Buoyancy-driven glider control. Ultra-low-power flight using only buoyancy changes and a rudder, for multi-month, trans-oceanic missions.
tpt-t-marine-tether: ROV tether management system (TMS). Real-time catenary physics simulation to prevent tether snagging, tension monitoring, and winch control.
Environment & Perception
tpt-t-marine-current: Ocean current modeling. Reads ADCP (Acoustic Doppler Current Profiler) data and predicts drift for AUVs and ASVs.
tpt-t-marine-tide: Tidal prediction and tidal stream modeling. Uses harmonic constituents to predict currents at any point, critical for ROV operations near structures.
tpt-t-marine-sonar: Multibeam, sidescan, and forward-looking sonar processing. SIMD-accelerated beamforming and zero-copy point cloud generation.
tpt-t-marine-pressure: Depth/pressure sensor management. Handles temperature compensation, hysteresis correction, and leak detection via pressure differential monitoring.
Safety & Comms
tpt-t-marine-safety: Flooding detection, emergency buoyancy (dropping weights / inflating bladders), thermal shutdown, and "lost vehicle" procedures (ascending to surface after timeout).
tpt-t-marine-comms: Multi-modal link manager. Seamlessly transitions between acoustic (underwater), RF (surface), cellular (near shore), and satellite (open ocean). Implements custom store-and-forward for when all links are down.
tpt-t-marine-colregs: COLREGS (International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea) compliance for ASVs. Deterministic decision-making for give-way vs. stand-on situations.
Offshore & Infrastructure
tpt-t-marine-offshore: Wind turbine foundation inspection, subsea cable routing, oil & gas wellhead monitoring.
tpt-t-marine-aquaculture: Fish cage net inspection, biomass estimation via hydroacoustics, mortality detection.
4. Extreme Performance Tactics (Marine-Specific)
The ocean environment forces performance decisions that no other domain faces.
4.1. The 100-bps Acoustic Protocol
Acoustic modems typically offer 100 bps to 10 kbps, with 500ms-2s latency and 30-60% packet loss. Standard TCP/IP is completely unusable here.
The Solution: tpt-t-marine-acoustic implements a custom Adaptive ARQ (Automatic Repeat reQuest) protocol. It uses a sliding window with forward error correction (Reed-Solomon) tuned to the measured packet loss rate. Mission-critical commands (e.g., "abort, surface now") are sent with triple-redundancy. Telemetry is heavily delta-compressed using rkyv—only state changes are transmitted.
Zero-Allocation: The entire protocol stack runs in a pre-allocated 4KB buffer. No heap, no Vec, no String.
4.2. SIMD-Accelerated INS/DVL Fusion
An AUV without GPS relies entirely on dead reckoning from its IMU and DVL. The EKF (Extended Kalman Filter) must run at 200Hz to prevent drift.
The Solution: tpt-t-marine-nav uses portable-simd to vectorize the matrix multiplications in the EKF prediction step. The 15-state filter (position, velocity, attitude, biases) executes in <200 microseconds on a Cortex-A72, leaving the CPU free for mission logic.
Zero-Copy Sensor Fusion: IMU and DVL packets are parsed directly from serial DMA buffers into the filter's state vector via rkyv casting.
4.3. Zero-Allocation Multibeam Sonar
A multibeam echosounder generates 512 beams per ping, at 20Hz, producing ~10,000 points/second. Standard point cloud libraries allocate on every ping.
The Solution: tpt-t-marine-sonar uses a slab-allocated ring buffer of fixed-size point clouds. Each ping overwrites the oldest cloud. SIMD is used to apply sound-velocity corrections and ray-tracing in parallel across all 512 beams. The result is a deterministic 2ms per ping, regardless of scene complexity.
4.4. Real-Time Tether Catenary Simulation
An ROV tether in a 2-knot current forms a complex 3D catenary. If the ROV moves unpredictably, the tether can snag on subsea structures.
The Solution: tpt-t-marine-tether models the tether as a lumped-mass chain (50 nodes) solved via Verlet integration. The simulation runs at 100Hz on a dedicated pinned core, predicting tether shape 5 seconds into the future. If a snag is predicted, it alerts the ROV pilot via tpt-teleop to adjust course.
4.5. COLREGS as Deterministic State Machine
Maritime law (COLREGS) defines 30+ rules for vessel interaction. An ASV must comply legally.
The Solution: tpt-t-marine-colregs encodes the rules as a deterministic finite state machine, not a neural network. Every decision (give-way, stand-on, sound whistle signal) is traceable, auditable, and legally defensible. The entire rule evaluation runs in <50 microseconds per detected vessel.
5. The "Marine Operations" Feature Matrix (v1)
5.1. AUV Mission Execution
Adaptive Sampling: The AUV detects a thermocline or plume. It autonomously modifies its mission to spend more time sampling that feature, within battery and time budgets.
Comms-Triggered Behaviors: When the AUV surfaces, it establishes a satellite link and transmits a compressed summary. The shore operator can send new waypoints or abort commands before the next dive.
Sleep-Wake Power Management: For multi-month deployments, the AUV sleeps 99% of the time, waking only for scheduled profiles or acoustic page-up calls from the surface buoy.
5.2. ROV Operations with Human-in-the-Loop
Seamless Teleoperation: Integrates directly with tpt-teleop for pilot control. The pilot's joystick inputs are translated into 6DOF thruster commands with rate limiting and collision avoidance.
Tether-Aware Motion: The ROV will not move in a direction that would increase tether snag risk, even if the pilot commands it. The pilot receives haptic feedback through the joystick when approaching tether limits.
Fiber-Optic Bandwidth Management: Over the tether's fiber link, tpt-t-marine-comms prioritizes control packets (<1ms) over video (H.265 hardware-encoded) over sonar data.
5.3. ASV Surface Operations
Dynamic Positioning (DP): The ASV holds position within 1 meter using GPS, IMU, and thruster feedback, even in 2-knot currents.
COLREGS Compliance: The ASV detects other vessels via AIS, radar, and cameras. It classifies the encounter (head-on, crossing, overtaking) and executes the correct COLREGS maneuver.
Wave Compensation: For offshore personnel transfer or crane operations, the ASV actively compensates for heave, pitch, and roll using its thrusters.
5.4. Hostile Environment Survival
Leak Detection: Every sealed compartment has a pressure sensor. tpt-t-marine-pressure detects a 0.1 PSI increase (indicating water ingress) and triggers emergency buoyancy in <100 milliseconds.
Biofouling Compensation: As barnacles grow on the hull, drag increases. tpt-t-marine-nav detects the increased drag signature and automatically derates mission speed to preserve battery.
Lost Vehicle Procedures: If the AUV loses all comms for >2 hours, it autonomously ascends, drifts on the surface, and transmits a GPS beacon via satellite until recovered.
6. The Complete "Subsea Pipeline Survey" Data Flow
Here is the lifecycle of a typical mission, demonstrating the zero-allocation flow across the stack:
The Mission Plan: The shore operator uploads a 50km pipeline survey route to the ASV via satellite. The ASV is carrying an AUV in its moon pool.
The Deployment: The ASV transits to the start point using tpt-t-marine-asv and tpt-t-marine-colregs. It dynamically positions over the pipeline start.
The Dive: The AUV is released. tpt-t-marine-buoyancy trims the vehicle neutral. tpt-t-marine-nav fuses the INS/DVL/pressure to establish a 3D position (GPS is now gone).
The Survey: The AUV follows the pipeline at 3 knots, 50m above the seabed. tpt-t-marine-sonar generates multibeam point clouds at 20Hz. tpt-t-marine-core runs edge AI to detect spans, anomalies, and debris.
The Acoustic Check-In: Every 30 minutes, the AUV ascends to 10m depth. tpt-t-marine-acoustic transmits a 200-byte status report to the ASV at 1200 bps. The ASV relays via satellite to shore.
The Adaptive Decision: The edge AI detects a potential pipeline anomaly. tpt-t-marine-auv modifies the mission to spend an extra 15 minutes doing a high-res sidescan pass.
The Recovery: Mission complete. The AUV surfaces, establishes a high-bandwidth WiFi link with the ASV, and transfers 50GB of sonar data in 20 minutes. The ASV reels the AUV back into the moon pool.
The Handoff: The ASV returns to port. The data is transferred to shore, where engineers review the anomalies flagged by the AUV's edge AI.

8. Developer Experience & Simulation
Testing underwater vehicles requires access to the ocean, which is expensive and weather-dependent. tpt-teleop-marine includes a comprehensive simulation stack.
tpt-t-marine-sim: A headless simulator that models:
6DOF vehicle dynamics with hydrodynamic drag coefficients
Realistic acoustic channel models (multi-path, loss, latency)
Ocean current fields (user-defined or from HYCOM/ROMS ocean models)
Tidal harmonics
Sonar returns from synthetic seabed geometries
Visualizer: A lightweight egui (MIT) binary that renders:
3D vehicle trajectories with depth color-coding
Acoustic link quality heatmaps
Tether catenary visualization
Multibeam sonar point clouds
COLREGS encounter scenarios for ASV testing
The simulator runs at 100x real-time, allowing developers to test a 30-day AUV mission in 7 hours of wall-clock time.
9. Conclusion
tpt-teleop-marine v1 brings the tpt ecosystem's uncompromising philosophy to the most challenging robotics environment on Earth.
By writing a custom 100-bps acoustic protocol, fusing INS/DVL with SIMD-accelerated Kalman filters, processing multibeam sonar in slab-allocated ring buffers, and encoding COLREGS as a deterministic state machine, tpt-teleop-marine proves that you don't need heavy, cloud-dependent middleware to operate in the deep ocean.
It is a battle-ready, commercially unencumbered operating system for the future of offshore wind, subsea infrastructure, scientific oceanography, and maritime defense—capable of running on a $500 AUV or a $50M ROV, from the surface to 6000 meters depth, all while maintaining the strict MIT-only dependency chain that defines the tpt ecosystem.

tpt-teleop-marine → tpt-teleop Integration
Adapter Crate: tpt-t-marine-teleop (new crate in the marine repo)
Integration Flow:
An ROV is inspecting a subsea pipeline. The pilot is on a surface vessel.
tpt-teleop provides the primary teleoperation link: joystick → ROV thrusters, video feed → pilot's monitor.
tpt-t-marine-teleop receives ControlCommand and translates it into 6DOF thruster commands (e.g., "thrust forward at 10%, yaw left at 5%").
The pilot also controls the ROV's manipulator arm via a second joystick or VR gloves.
tpt-t-marine-tether monitors tether tension and overrides the pilot's commands if a snag is detected.
Domain-Specific Adaptation:
Acoustic Comms Fallback: If the fiber-optic tether is cut, tpt-t-marine-teleop switches to acoustic control via tpt-t-marine-acoustic, reducing the control rate to 1Hz but maintaining critical command capability.
Buoyancy Compensation: The adapter automatically compensates for the ROV's buoyancy changes as it deploys tools or collects samples.
Tether-Aware Motion: The adapter prevents the pilot from commanding movements that would increase tether snag risk.
